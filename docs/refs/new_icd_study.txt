Previous studies have strongly suggested that crystallizing out pure water from solutions through

freezing may provide an efficient means of wastewater purification. In that regard, freeze crys-
tallization is nowadays perceived as an edge-cutting technology for industrial concentration and

currently being developed for application in areas such as wastewater treatment. Despite these
developments, the kinetics involved have not yet been fully registered. For instance the changes

brought by operating conditions and environmental factors such as temperature, humidity, radia-
tion, and wind flow patterns. We have modeled natural freezing of water under simulated winter

environment with an aim of understanding the underlying kinetics of a wastewater treatment pro-
cess based on water layer crystallization. It is generally established that ice growth is driven by

the heat transfer into and out of the liquid surface due to the evolving temperature gradient. This
however does not always provide a way forward if the temperature is not the only influential factor,
and that calls for studies involving other environmental conditions. In this case for instance, heat
transfer is in turn affected by the wind flow patters, radiation, relative humidity, and the resulting

vapor pressure differences. The overall effect is a continuous evolution in the vapor pressure gra-
dient around the gas-liquid interface that greatly influence ice growth. We have derived accurate

self-limiting models for direct correlation between the vapor pressure gradient, ∂p/∂z, the ice grain
boundary migration acceleration, a and eventually growth rate, G in ms−1

. Upon experimental
validation and error analysis the numerical model, ρa + ∇P = 0 returned a minimal relative error
of only 0.35%. In addition, ice samples produced from a local peat production settlement pond did
not show significant physical observations different from samples produced from a fresh water lake,
thus pointing to the fact that a significant achievement has been achieved with regards to physical
water treatment (by this method), an important preliminary stage for water purification.
